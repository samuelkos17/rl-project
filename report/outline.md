## 1. Introduction and research question  [Samuel]
Does the choice of exploration strategy matter in DQN, and can state coverage
explain why? State H1, H2, H3 and what would confirm each.

- H1: early coverage predicts final return within an instance, and the effect
  grows with difficulty. Confirmed by `confirms_h1` in `report/results.md`.
- H2: task-relevant coverage predicts better than raw coverage. Confirmed by
  `confirms_h2` -- which needs a larger correlation *and* non-overlapping CIs.
- H3: the strategy ranking changes as difficulty rises. Read off the Kendall
  tau column of the rank-stability table.

## 2. Background  [Samuel]
DQN, Double DQN and why we fixed it (overestimated Q-values act as an accidental
exploration bonus and would contaminate the comparison), the
exploration/exploitation trade-off, MiniGrid and partial observability.

## 4. Experimental setup  [Samuel]
13 instances across 3 families, the difficulty axis, 5 seeds, fixed
hyperparameters, fixed step budget, pinned layouts, greedy extrinsic-only
evaluation with `eval_episodes = 1`. Table of hyperparameters.

Numbers: the header block of `report/results.md` (runs analysed, instances,
strategies, seeds per configuration).

## 8. Limitations  [Daniel]
Perceptual aliasing in the count-based bonus (two different places can look
identical in a 7x7x3 view, so they share a count). Pinned layouts mean we study
single-maze exploration, not generalisation. One DQN variant. Fixed
hyperparameters, not tuned per strategy. Fixed step budget means hard instances
may be budget-limited rather than genuinely unsolvable.
